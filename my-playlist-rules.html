Rules for making a playlist

Posted on Aug 02, 2026

Curating a collection of music for a loved one to listen to is a time-honoured tradition, becoming accessible and a huge part of pop culture around the time cassette tapes and home-recording became possible. With the rise of music streaming, we mostly make playlists on Deezer or whatever now, sending a link to it instead of transferring a physical object. I deeply enjoy making playlists, so I’ve had a lot of time to think about what I think about when I make one.

Jacek Halicki, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

I got new rules I count 'em

  • Only one song per artist. I have favourites sometimes, and this prevents me from just stuffing the playlist with their songs.
  • It either has a specific mood: that is, it tries to evoke a certain emotion. If it is intended to switch between emotions, the shifts should be gradual and smooth.
  • Medium length, between 20 to 45 minutes. I want the recipient to be able to listen to it all at once, and feel satisfied if they do.

These rules are obviously not concrete. You can (and I do) break them for effect. A short playlist could be used to convey something really specific. A long playlist could be used to portray a more general mood, or just act as a soundtrack to some other task (e.g. for a road-trip). Artist-specific playlists can obviously exist, as a sort of Greatest Hits compilation.

A playlist
