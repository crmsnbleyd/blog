Arbitrage is necessary but kind of impossible

Posted on Aug 22, 2024 in blogpost

I’m autistic, and every other month I get kind of into a new topic to such an extent that I do not find joy in doing anything else. Lately, it has been markets and macro-economics (boo!). I’ve read a couple of interesting books about Indian markets and markets in general, and my blog has been languishing from lack of attention, so here we go.

Dictionary definition

For economists living in their ivory towers, arbitrage means taking advantage of difference in prices between markets to carry out an instantaneous transaction which nets you the difference as profit.

Say you have two markets, the Indian Currency Board and the National Currency Exchange. Both of these markets are electronic and any transactions are settled immediately without anyone having to move from their seats. In ICB, $USD can be purchased for 85 Indian rupees, while in NCE, the same currency can be purchased for 87 Indian rupees. A cheeky fellow can purchase Dollars for 85 rupees and sell them immediately for 87 rupees, netting a profit of 2 rupees per transaction.

The immediacy of this pair of transactions is really important, as arbitrage is a risk-free strategy. If you wait even for a couple of minutes, the price of Dollars in both markets could decrease, netting you a loss!

Law of one price

Ideal markets are arbitrage-free, which means that every market would have the same price for the same goods, otherwise it would be really silly for things that are exactly the same to have different costs depending on what option from a drop-down you select.

Market friction

However, if you have looked at any service that can be bought for money, you know there’s a whole bunch of charges, including taxes. Both the markets might charge a fixed transaction fee or one based on the transaction volume (amount of money spent), which would make arbitrage with small gains actually lose money.

Like the physicists and their spherical cows, economists have created the convenient abstraction of the frictionless market, and mostly spend their days creating fantasy models based on this idea.
