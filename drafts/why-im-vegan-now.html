Why it took me a while to go vegan

Posted on Jul 31, 2026 in blogpost

The reasons to become vegan are well-known, and it’s not as hard to go vegan as one would think. This post is specifically about why I never went vegan earlier than when I actually did.

  • I did not grow up in a vegetarian family, and the inherent casteism of Indian vegetarianism[1] (it’s mostly about ritual purity rather than actual care for animals, since they’d be vegan otherwise) alienated me from vegetarianism, and by extension, veganism.
  • If you start thinking about suffering from a utilitarian perspective, there is almost nothing you can do to not be involved in it, at least indirectly. There is intense slavery in the globalised supply chain[2], for example, and if one wished to clean their hands of everything wrong with the world, there’s not much they could do.
  • I always thought people positioning veganism as the only moral option was unfair on indigenous people who live(d) sustainably as part of their surroundings, rather than engaging in destructive practices.
  • I had trouble eating vegetables when I was younger. This might be because I hated the way they were cooked at home.
  • Vegan food is still niche in India where even though there’s a sizeable vegetarian population, dairy is everywhere. I think if you tell the average Indian you don’t consume dairy they’re going to think you’re crazy.
  • It felt like an elitist and stupid thing to care about amongst all the suffering in the world.

Here’s what I think now:

Being vegan for me is specifically about not being complicit in unnecessary animal deaths. It is okay to decide what I wish to eat on my short life on this planet. I don’t want to eat animals and I don’t want them to be grown for food, when perfectly serviceable alternatives exist. I no longer feel like have to solve all the issues in the world to care about this one thing, and suffer through doing something that fundamentally makes me uncomfortable, just because I didn’t want to seem uncool.
