Interesting things I learned on the job

Posted on Jan 16, 2024 in blogpost

Before starting my current job at Infoworks as a DevOps Engineer, I'd never had a reason to do interactive command line usage quickly or write a shell script. Sure, I was a Linux user and could get around on my own, but I wasn't really a Bash user in the sense that I used the features the shell offered other than just using it to type commands in. - abc{1, 2} - C-r

A useful book

I'm currently reading a book on bash. It's pretty good. I heard of it on this blog post on fabiensanglard.net. I haven't cribbed from it for this post, I promise, but it has a lot more features than I've mentioned here!

The cloud

I have learned so much about the cloud since starting at my role.

Before this, my only practical exposure was through Google training through my university's Google student developer club (sounds pretty dystopian, does it not?), which you had to do for 30 days and then you'd get some free swag. It was awful and I quit after a couple weeks.

Collaboration is great

You can learn a lot of stuff working with other people, and even just observing them work! I had been a loner since childhood so it wasn't until I was contractually obligated to hang out with others that I discovered this. I suppose this is a well-known thing, but the rate of improvement is baffling. I have also seen my interest in programming and computing increase ever since starting this job, and that has directly led to me getting better at it.
Sidebar: I follow a lot of tech-y people on the fediverse, and that has really helped as well, thank you if you're one of them!
