Nerd consumerism

Posted on Aug 02, 2026

Outline

I. Introduction: The Saturation Paradox and the Pre-Trip Spiral

  • The Context (Bengaluru vs. Jakarta): Living in India, where the used device market is notoriously thin, wildly overpriced, and lacking niche variety.
  • The Catalyst: An upcoming trip to Indonesia—a playground for imported Japanese second-hand electronics, pristine vintage audio, and retro handhelds. Pre-trip window shopping triggers a full-blown "device acquisition spiral."
  • The Irony of the Laptop: Recalling the deliberate decision to sell off a heavy gaming laptop specifically to simplify life, eliminate friction, and focus on actually finishing games on the Switch OLED.
  • The Current Reality: Sitting on a pristine setup—Switch OLED, a phone running native Steam games, a high-res DAP, and a turntable—backed by a massive, untouched software backlog.
  • The Glitch: Browser tabs multiplied exponentially anyway: vintage portable CD players, Switch 2 spec leaks, PC handhelds like the Lenovo Legion Go S, and a cheap PS4 (which is actually not cheap anymore thanks to the ongoing RAM crisis) purely to run Bloodborne.
  • Thesis: Tech consumerism in enthusiast circles is rarely about solving a functional gap; it’s an obsession with form factors, novelty, and the false promise that new hardware will somehow fix our broken relationship with our media backlog.

II. The Hardware Solution to a Software Problem

The "Fresh Start" Delusion: The belief that buying a new handheld (like a Legion Go S or Switch 2) will somehow magically motivate us to finish the 50+ Steam/Switch games sitting untouched in our existing library. Form Factor Fetishism: Why we spend hours researching device ergonomics, chipsets, and screen tech instead of actually launching a game from our current library. The "Niche Use Case" Justifications: How the brain creates artificial boundary lines to validate duplicate gear: Music: "The turntable is for home, the DAP is for lossless travel... but a portable CD player gives me physical media on the go." Gaming: "The Switch OLED is for Nintendo games, the phone is for native Steam ports... but a dedicated PC handheld would feel more 'legitimate', and a PS4 is required for Bloodborne."

III part A weaponised nostalgia and FOMO

I miss my PSP I feel like I missed out on the DS and 3DS

III. The Dopamine Architecture: Setup over Gameplay

Configuring vs. Playing: Spending 3 hours tweaking settings, installing emulators, or curating libraries, only to close the device once it's "ready." The Hunt vs. The Capture: Why watching review videos, comparing spec sheets (e.g., Z1 Extreme vs. custom ARM chips), and tracking hardware leaks yields more dopamine than making progress on a massive backlog. The Post-Unboxing Decay: The speed at which a shiny new device turns into just another screen sitting next to the Switch OLED with an unplayed game on the pause menu.

IV. Breaking the Cycle: Software First, Hardware Last

The Backlog Audit: Shifting focus from "what device do I need next?" to "what incredible experiences am I currently ignoring on the gear I already own?" The "Finish Three First" Rule: Enforcing a personal constraint: No new hardware research or purchases until you complete three games from your existing Switch/Steam backlog and listen to three full albums straight through. Reclaiming Gratitude: Learning to see current hardware not as "outdated tools waiting to be upgraded," but as fully capable gateways to games you haven't explored yet.

There will always be time as new devices come out, RAM and storage get cheap again
